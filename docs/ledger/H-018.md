## Question
Is the second PPO epoch worth its cost? Epochs here means optimizer steps per iteration: `Learn()` accumulates the gradient over all minibatches of an epoch (miniBatch 25k, batch 400k) and takes ONE `StepOptims()` per epoch, so `epochs = 2` is 2 nudges per 400k rows.
Measured on the live ts2 control (2026-11-23, last 150 iterations): mean KL 0.0010, clip fraction 0.24%, policy update magnitude 0.36 per iteration. The trust-region clip is nowhere near binding, so the second epoch is not being held back by it.
Learn-pass timers (new, `Learn Phase/*`): forward + loss 1,621 ms, backward 2,828 ms, clip 5 ms, optimizer 73 ms of a 5.03 s pass; learn is the longer leg of the pipelined iteration (collection 6.5 s mean, consumption 5.9 s).

## Design (user-directed 2026-11-23, to run after the architecture chain finishes)
Fresh `ts2` start, arm `ts2ep1` (to be added to `ConfigureGCOFork` as `ts2` + `cfg.ppo.epochs = 1`, whitelist `--gco-fork-ts2ep1`). **Everything else identical to H-014**: seed 123, tickSkip 2 / delay 1, horizons x4, lambda 0.9875, Nexto+Chronos 0.30, near-goal mix, networks of the control, batch 400k, miniBatch 25k, policy LR 1.5e-4. One lever.
Mix schedule and run length follow the architecture chain: near-goal mix to 4B steps, kickoff-dense mix after (KICK_MIX marker), to 5B. The learning-rate schedule is unchanged, so the run sees HALF the optimizer steps per row of the control.
Control: H-014's `ts2` (resumed from its 1.0018B pre-switch copy; its first 1.0018B steps are the same fresh start, so the comparison is clean on the common prefix, with the control read at its own wall-break step).

## Pre-registered predictions (written before the run)
1. **Learn pass and throughput.** `Learn Phase/*` falls by about 45-50% (about 2.6 s of 5.0 s). Overall steps/s rises 1.2-1.4x, not 2x, because collection (6.5 s) becomes the longer leg.
2. **Policy movement.** `Policy Update Magnitude` about 0.18 (half), `Mean KL Divergence` about 0.0005, clip fraction lower.
3. **Wall break per step.** Later than the control's: 1.3-2x more steps. Reason: at fixed LR each nudge moves the weights a roughly fixed amount, so half the nudges is about half the progress per iteration, and the rare goal signal is data-limited only in part.
4. **Wall break per wall-clock hour.** Within +-25% of the control, because the cheaper iterations roughly pay for the extra iterations needed. A result outside that band either way is the finding.
5. **Entropy** stays inside 0.77 +- 30% before the wall.

## Readouts
- Wall step: the step at which 4 x the no-touch penalty exceeds -0.0005 over a 100M window (`ts2_read.py --check`, as in H-014). The seed spread of that step across fresh 15 Hz runs was 580-860M (about +-20%), so a shift under about 25% is unresolved; a 1.3-2x shift is resolvable even single-seed.
- Wall-clock hours to the same step, from `Overall Steps/Second` and the logged times.
- Gauntlet (1024 games, four bots) and the cross-architecture-style head-to-head against the control's final checkpoint (same architecture, so the plain `GGL_H2H_OPP=<dir>` form works).

## Decision rule (pre-registered)
- Faster per wall-clock hour by more than 25% and no worse than 1.5x slower per step: adopt 1 epoch for future long runs, one lever at a time, and record the change in `docs/REWARD_CHANGE_LOG.md`-style (config) notes.
- Wall-clock neutral (within +-25%): keep 2 epochs; the control is the more informative baseline for everything already run.
- Slower per hour by more than 25%: keep 2 epochs and drop the idea.
Do not combine with the LR-doubled 1-epoch variant or a 4-epoch variant in the same run; each is its own follow-up and its own entry.

## Guards and revert
- Stop and report if the wall check goes the wrong way for 1B steps past the control's window, if entropy leaves 0.77 +- 30% before the wall, or a crash loop. Its own folder `build/checkpoints_gco_fork_ts2ep1`; revert is stopping it (nothing is deleted).
- The `Learn Phase/*` panels are read in the first 100M steps to confirm prediction 1 early.

## Caveats
Single seed. The prediction that update size halves assumes each optimizer step moves the weights a roughly fixed amount; that has not been verified for this optimizer mix, and the first 100M steps check it. The pipelined collection means the policy that collects iteration N+1 is already one update behind; with one epoch the lag is one nudge instead of two, a small change in off-policy-ness that is a second, unavoidable difference between the arms.
